## Why Pay for Photoshop?

I've been down the Photoshop rabbit hole. Twenty-two bucks a month starts sounding reasonable when you're deep in a project, but step back and it's absurd — that's $264 a year just to crop photos and tweak colors. For freelancers, students, or anyone who doesn't edit images for a living, it's hard to justify.

The good news? Free editing tools have come a long way. I've tested dozens over the years, and the ones below genuinely rival Photoshop for most everyday work. Some even do things Photoshop can't.

## The 10 Best Free Alternatives

### 1. GIMP
Still the heavyweight champion after all these years. Layers, masks, brushes, color correction — GIMP does it all, and it runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux. The interface takes some getting used to, but there's a reason every "free Photoshop alternative" list starts here.

### 2. Photopea
This one blows my mind. It runs entirely in your browser, supports PSD files natively (yes, real Photoshop files), and you don't need to install anything. I've used it on library computers, friend's laptops, even my phone in a pinch. For quick edits, it's my go-to.

### 3. Krita
Built for digital painting first, but don't let that fool you — its brush engine is best in class, and it handles photo editing beautifully. If you do any kind of illustration work alongside photo editing, this is your tool.

### 4. Paint.NET
Simple doesn't mean weak. Paint.NET strips away the complexity and gives you exactly what you need: layers, effects, and a clean interface. Perfect if GIMP feels like piloting a 747 when all you need is a bicycle.

### 5. Canva (Free Tier)
Canva's not really a Photoshop replacement in the traditional sense, but for social media graphics, presentations, and quick designs, nothing beats it. Thousands of templates mean you don't start from a blank canvas every time.

### 6. Pixlr
Another browser-based editor that's been around forever. Two versions: Pixlr X for beginners (drag and drop, very intuitive) and Pixlr E for when you need more control. Great fallback if Photopea's down.

### 7. Inkscape
Vector graphics, not raster — think Illustrator, not Photoshop. But if your workflow includes logos, illustrations, or scalable designs, Inkscape is indispensable. Pairs beautifully with GIMP.

### 8. Darktable
Photographers, this one's for you. Darktable is Lightroom done free. RAW processing, non-destructive editing, color grading — it's replaced Lightroom for a lot of serious photographers I know.

### 9. RawTherapee
Similar to Darktable but with a different philosophy. Some algorithms here produce better results for certain types of photos. I keep both installed and switch depending on what I'm working on.

### 10. PhotoScape X
The Swiss Army knife of photo editors. Batch processing, GIF creator, screen capture, RAW converter — it does everything. Not the best at any one thing, but incredibly useful to have around.

## Which One Should You Choose?

Honest answer: start with Photopea if you want something that just works in a browser. Download GIMP if you need serious editing power. Grab Canva if your work is mostly social media graphics. The rest you'll discover naturally as your needs grow.

| Tool | Best For | Platform |
|------|----------|----------|
| GIMP | Advanced editing | Windows/Mac/Linux |
| Photopea | Quick browser edits | Web |
| Krita | Digital painting | Windows/Mac/Linux |
| Canva | Social media | Web |
