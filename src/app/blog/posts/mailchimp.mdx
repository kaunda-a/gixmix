## QR Codes in 2026

I remember when QR codes were a novelty that nobody actually scanned. Then the pandemic hit, and suddenly every restaurant table had one, every store had them on the door, and we all got very comfortable pointing our phones at little black-and-white squares.

They never went away. QR codes are now permanent fixtures — restaurants, stores, business cards, advertisements, even gravestones (seriously). And they've gotten more powerful than ever.

**Tips that actually boost scan rates**:

- **Custom colors**: Match your brand — QR codes in brand colors get more scans than generic black-and-white ones
- **Logo overlay**: Drop your logo in the center. It works, and it builds brand recognition

## Error Correction Levels

This is the part most people skip, and it matters more than you'd think.

| Level | Recovery | Best For |
|-------|----------|----------|
| L | 7% | High-quality prints (no damage expected) |
| M | 15% | General use — the safe default |
| Q | 25% | When you're adding a logo overlay |
| H | 30% | Damaged surfaces or curved packaging |

Higher error correction means the QR code can be partially damaged or obscured and still scan. The tradeoff is a denser pattern, so don't use H for everything — M is fine for most cases.
